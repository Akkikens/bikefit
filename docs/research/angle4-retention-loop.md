## Competitor landscape (what was actually read)

### MyVeloFit (web app, not on the App Store)
- Home: https://www.myvelofit.com/ . Browser based. "Record and upload a video of yourself pedalling on a bike set up on a trainer or rollers, or on a smart bike or spin/exercise bike." Features named: mobility assessment, "AI body scan", "Tracking & Progress". Bike types in alt text: time trial, road, mountain. "30-Day Better Fit Guarantee". Fit is described as iterative ("repeat steps 3 through 5 several times").
- Pricing: https://www.myvelofit.com/pricing . Starter $0 (one fit upload per week, single bike, basic analysis). Enthusiast $35.00 USD once for 2 weeks (5 uploads per 24 h, single bike). Pro $75.00 USD per year ("Unlimited fitting for all your bikes, year round", unlimited bike profiles and uploads). Expert Review add on "Starting from $75USD".
- FAQ: https://www.myvelofit.com/faq . "MyVeloFit is a web app, so you won't find us in the app store." Results take "approximately 90 seconds". Camera guidance: "We need to see your entire body throughout the full pedal stroke, including your head, feet, arms and legs." and "Be sure you place your camera consistently relative to your bike on both sides to ensure consistent results." Lists reasons to recheck: new pain, changed fitness, changed mobility, injury, new equipment. No dedicated compare over time feature described. Processing location not stated (upload to website implies server side).
- Takeaway for angle 4: MyVeloFit already sells the "year round, all your bikes" idea as its Pro tier, but as an upload count, not as a tracking product. Its own FAQ names the come back triggers (pain, fitness, mobility, injury, equipment) without building a loop around them.

### Bike Fast Fit (iOS, three apps)
- Home: https://www.bikefastfit.com/ . Side on iPhone on a trainer, short video, markerless, "240 frames per second" on supported devices. Recommendations as ranges: "a correct riding position is a range the rider settles into." US prices per year: EZ $9.99 (one week trial), Elite $29.99, Pro $139.99.
- Compare: https://www.bikefastfit.com/compare . EZ: side view only, 4 second recording, knee, foot, torso, shoulder angles, crank fixed at 172.5 mm, no history ("Session name only" is Elite). Elite: adds ankle and hip angles, front view knee tracking, "Compare two recordings", sessions, before and after video report. Pro: LiveCheck real time, rear view (pelvis and balance), custom target ranges, "Initial versus final comparison", CSV export, iCloud sync about 10x capacity. All tiers have iCloud sync and free BFF Academy.
- Academy: https://academy.bikefastfit.com/ and https://academy.bikefastfit.com/en/ez/ . EZ lessons: "Why the 'right' bike fit is a range, not a number", "How to record well", "Saddle height", "Saddle fore/aft", "Upper body position", "The iterative fit process" with "The fit loop, in order" and "Adjust slowly, and know when you're done". No numeric ranges published on those pages. No cadence for rechecking stated.
- Takeaway: the strongest direct iOS competitor. History and compare exist but are gated behind $29.99 per year and framed as fitter tooling (sessions, records, branded reports). Nothing is framed around the rider's calendar (travel, season, change).

### Retul (Specialized, in studio)
- https://www.retul.com/ and https://www.retul.com/retul-fit . "Data Driven, Personally Crafted." LED markers on 8 anatomical points, Retul Vantage 3D motion capture, "real-time, three-dimensional data from each pedal stroke", final position documented with the Retul Zin tool, a report "you can refer to any time after your appointment". No price on the site. No follow up or re-fit product described.
- Takeaway: the Retul report is a static artifact. The gap is what happens to that position after the rider leaves the studio: the bike gets boxed, the saddle slips, the rider changes. A phone app that treats the studio report as a baseline to protect is complementary, not competitive.

## Apple Vision body pose (primary docs)
- VNDetectHumanBodyPoseRequest: https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/vision/vndetecthumanbodyposerequest.json . "A request that detects a human body pose." Available iOS 14.0+, visionOS 1.0+. 2D, normalized points, per point confidence (WWDC20).
- WWDC20 "Detect Body and Hand Pose with Vision": https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10653/ . Landmarks: nose, eyes, ears, neck, shoulders, elbows, wrists, root, hips, knees, ankles. Limitations quoted: "If the people on the scene are bent over or upside down, the body pose algorithm will not perform as well." "the results may get worse if the subject is close to the edges of the screen." "the pose might not be determinable due to obstructive flowing clothing." Vision gives a confidence per point, ARKit body pose does not. Relevance: a rider in an aero tuck is partly "bent over", so TT captures need a validation pass and visible rejection when confidence drops.
- VNDetectHumanBodyPose3DRequest: https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/vision/vndetecthumanbodypose3drequest.json . "A request that detects points on human bodies in 3D space, relative to the camera." iOS 17.0+. "If the system allows it, the request uses AVDepthData information to improve the accuracy."
- VNHumanBodyPose3DObservation: https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/vision/vnhumanbodypose3dobservation.json . bodyHeight "The estimated human body height, in meters." heightEstimation, cameraOriginMatrix "A transform from the skeleton hip to the camera.", pointInImage, cameraRelativePosition.
- Joint names (17): https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/vision/vnhumanbodypose3dobservation/jointname.json . topHead, centerHead, centerShoulder, leftShoulder, rightShoulder, leftElbow, rightElbow, leftWrist, rightWrist, leftHip, rightHip, leftKnee, rightKnee, leftAnkle, rightAnkle, root, spine. Note: no foot or toe joint in 3D, and no eyes or ears, so ankle angle and head position need the 2D request or a separate heuristic.
- WWDC23 "Explore 3D body pose and person segmentation in Vision": https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/111241/ . "returns a 3D skeleton with 17 joints". Positions "returned in meters relative to the captured scene in the real world with an origin at a root joint." bodyHeight is "either a more accurate measured height or a reference height of 1.8 meters" depending on depth metadata. "This initial revision returns one skeleton for the most prominent person detected in the frame." "LiDAR is powerful because it allows for accurate scale and measurement of the scene."
- Design consequence: joint angles (knee, hip, torso, elbow) are unit free and come from the 2D request on any iPhone running iOS 14 or later. Millimetre scale (saddle height in mm) is only trustworthy with LiDAR depth and even then should be cross checked against a user entered reference such as crank length or wheel diameter. Therefore the product reports angles as the primary truth and bike measurements as user entered values.

## Apple policy pages read
- App privacy details: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/ . "Data that is processed only on device is not 'collected' and does not need to be disclosed in your answers." Collect means "transmitting data off the device in a way that allows you and/or your third-party partners to access it for a period longer than what is necessary to service the transmitted request in real time." Supports a Data Not Collected label for an all on device app with no analytics SDK.
- Review guidelines: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ . 1.4.1: "Medical apps that could provide inaccurate data or information, or that could be used for diagnosing or treating patients may be reviewed with greater scrutiny." and "Apps should remind users to check with a doctor in addition to using the app and before making medical decisions." 4.3(b): "Don't submit apps that are indistinguishable from what's already widely available." 5.1.3 covers health data use. Design consequence: no pain diagnosis, no injury claims, no "prevents injury" copy; the app measures posture on a bike and logs bike setup, and tells people with pain to see a fitter or clinician.

## Concept notes (full text is in the structured result)
Working name candidates: Setline, Fitdrift, Kneeline, Saddlemark, Rebase. Core idea: the first capture is a baseline, every later capture is a comparison to that baseline, and the app only asks the rider to come back when the rider's own calendar gives a reason: a change was made, the bike travelled, the season flipped, or a monthly drift check is due. Noise floor is measured per rider from two back to back captures on day one, so drift is only flagged when it exceeds that rider's own repeatability.

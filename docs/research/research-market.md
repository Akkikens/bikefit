# Market size research: consumer AI bike fit app (US, Europe, Asia)

Researched 2026-11-03. Every number below carries the URL it was read from. "Sourced" means read from the cited page. "Assumption" means my own estimate. Web search budget ran out before Australia national, Korea, Singapore and gravel specific numbers could be confirmed; see Open questions.

## 1. Triathlon population

### USA Triathlon
- 2024 Impact Report: "302K+ active, unique members in 2024", up 1.6% over 2023, below 329K in 2019. New members were "nearly 50% of membership sales in 2024". Ages 20 to 29 grew by 10,000+; ages 30 to 39 became the largest cohort. Adult race finishes in ranked competitive races "280K+ in 2024" down from 287K in 2023. 825 unique adult multisport events, 209 youth events, over 3,600 races.
  https://www.usatriathlon.org/articles/news/usa-triathlon-2024-impact-report-a-year-of-sport-celebration-and-community
- 2025 Impact Report (released Feb 2026): "303,000 unique active members in 2025"; ages 20 to 39 the largest demographic; 20 to 29 group grew nearly 20% with two thirds new to the sport; 829 sanctioned events (824 in 2024); 3,792 total races; 73% of participants raced Super Sprint, Sprint or Olympic.
  https://www.usatriathlon.org/news/2026/february/04/usa-triathlon-releases-2025-impact-report-highlighting-strategic-growth-membership-event-momentum-and-progress-toward-la28
- Slowtwitch on the 2024 report: 302,000 adults and youths with multi year, annual or one day memberships; Cal Tri Events cites a 46% decline in USAT finishers and 40% decline in races from peak.
  https://slowtwitch.com/news/2024-us-triathlon-participation-good-news-and-bad-news/
- Slowtwitch on USAT finances: annual memberships brought in $7.48 million in 2022; total revenue over $18 million in 2022; annual silver $60, gold $99, platinum $400.
  https://www.slowtwitch.com/news/usa-triathlons-massive-membership-mistake/
- Note: "unique active members" mixes annual and one day members. Assumption: a majority of the 302K are one day or sprint focused; the core "owns a tri or road bike and trains year round" segment is likely 100K to 150K in the US (assumption, not sourced).

Treat all as low credibility paid report marketing.

- Only fitter side primary data: Jon Iriberri 2017 survey of about 200 fitters in 38 nations: average fit price around $200 (range $100 to $300), session slightly over 2 hours, average around 250 fits per year per fitter, average technology spend $6,000, nearly 60% of respondents from USA, England and Australia; typical customer is a competitive or recreational road rider, triathletes and TT riders "less common".
  https://cyclingindustry.news/market-study-bikefittings-key-demographics-service-charges-and-profit-potential
- Bottom up fitting services estimate (assumption): if 1,500 to 3,000 full time fitters worldwide each do 250 fits at $200, that is $75M to $150M per year of professional fit revenue, plus shop fits bundled with bike sales. Not a sourced figure.

## 4. Share of riders who have had a professional fit
- Peer reviewed: Priego Quesada et al., European Journal of Sport Science 2019 (PMID 30556469, DOI 10.1080/17461391.2018.1556738), international retrospective survey of 849 cyclists (Feb to Oct 2016), three groups: user bike fitting, professional bike fitting, no bike fitting. Professional fit associated with higher odds of no pain (OR 2.35, 95% CI 1.42 to 3.98); user fit same OR 2.35 (1.48 to 3.84); no association with injury. Group sizes not in the abstract.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30556469/
  https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU202001000344
- Slowtwitch 2005 thread: posters guessed 30 to 40% of triathletes properly fit, others "10% at the most". Anecdotal.
  https://forum.slowtwitch.com/t/of-athletes-with-proper-fit/324266
- No published survey percentage found. Assumption: 20 to 40% of serious triathletes and 10 to 20% of enthusiast road riders have paid for a professional fit; everyone else is the addressable "never fitted" pool.

## 9. TAM / SAM / SOM (bottom up)

People counts (ranges; S = sourced anchor, A = assumption):
- Triathletes who train year round: US 100K to 150K (A, from 302K USAT unique members S); Europe 300K to 400K licensed (S partial: FR 75K, DE 61K, GB 29K, ES 24K), 500K to 800K racing at least once (A); Japan about 30K registered (S 2019); Australia 15K to 25K (A); Ironman 250K registrations (S) within these. Core triathlete pool: 0.6M to 1.2M (A).
- Enthusiast road and gravel riders (own a drop bar bike, 50+ ride days): US 2M to 3.5M (A, 5 to 8% of 41.2M paved S); Europe 5M to 10M (A; DE 6M "frequent or occasional" S; England 6.1M leisure and sport cyclists S); Asia 2M to 5M (A; JP 9.3M hobby cyclists S, TW 5.11M frequent S). Enthusiast pool: 9M to 18M (A).
- TAM (people): 10M to 19M riders in US, Europe, Asia who could plausibly want a fit. TAM (dollars, at the pro fit reference price of $200 once every 3 years, A): $0.7B to $1.3B of fit demand per year; at a software price of $35/yr (MyVeloFit Pro is $75, single fit $35): $350M to $650M per year if everyone paid, which nobody will.
- SAM: iPhone owners in that pool who already use a training or tracking app. iOS share blended about 45% across US/UK/JP (50 to 54%) and DE (28%) (S); app users about 50% of enthusiasts (A, Strava 50M MAU S suggests a large overlap). SAM about 2.3M to 4.3M people. Triathlete only SAM: 0.6M to 1.2M x 50% iOS x 60% app users = 180K to 430K.
- SOM year 1 (free app, no marketing budget): comparable dedicated iOS bike fit apps have under 700 combined US ratings (S), which at 50 to 100 downloads per rating implies the whole category has done on the order of 35K to 70K US downloads ever (A). A realistic first year for a well made free app with organic ASO and a few forum and YouTube mentions: 10K to 50K downloads, 3K to 15K monthly actives at the end of year one (A). If a paid tier were ever switched on at RevenueCat H&F freemium medians (2.9% D35 download to paid, $35.64 RLTV per payer year one, S): 290 to 1,450 payers and $10K to $52K year one revenue. Top quartile (6.2%): $22K to $110K. This is below the $1K/mo bar that 82.7% of new apps also miss (S), so the "no revenue" constraint costs little in expectation.
- Sensitivity: the single most important unknown is the share of enthusiasts who will film themselves on a trainer; MyVeloFit's 5 years of existence with forum level traction and no native app suggests that share is small but nonzero.
